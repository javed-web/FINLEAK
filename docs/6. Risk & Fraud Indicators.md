# Stage 6 — Risk & Fraud Indicators

## 🎯 Objective

In this stage, I wanted to identify merchants that may require further investigation based on their actual transaction behavior.

Instead of creating a random `fraud_flag`, I decided to derive risk signals from the data already generated in FinLeak.

The idea is simple:

> **Don't label something as fraud without evidence. First identify suspicious behavior, then investigate it.**

---

## 💡 Why I Built This

At this point, FinLeak already had:

- Customer data
- Merchant data
- Transactions
- Payment attempts
- Refunds
- Chargebacks
- Transaction fees

This gave me enough information to start looking for behavioral risk patterns.

A merchant with a normal-looking predefined risk score may still have:

- unusually high transaction failures
- unusually high refund activity
- unusually high chargeback activity
- a high proportion of fraud-related chargebacks

So I wanted to combine these signals rather than depending on one risk score.

---

## 🔍 Risk Signals

I created five merchant-level risk signals.

### 1. High-risk merchant

Based on the existing merchant risk score:

```text
risk_score >= 70
```

This represents the baseline risk assigned to the merchant.

---

### 2. High failure rate

A merchant is flagged when:

```text
failure_rate > 10%
```

This helps identify merchants where payment transactions are failing unusually often.

---

### 3. High refund rate

A merchant is flagged when:

```text
refund_rate > 8%
```

This can indicate unusually high refund activity and therefore potential revenue leakage.

---

### 4. High chargeback rate

A merchant is flagged when:

```text
chargeback_rate > 2%
```

Chargebacks are particularly important because they represent disputed transactions and can create financial and operational risk.

---

### 5. Fraud-heavy merchant

A merchant is flagged when:

```text
fraud_chargeback_rate > 25%
```

This measures the percentage of the merchant's chargebacks that were associated with the `FRAUD` reason.

This does **not** mean the merchant itself committed fraud.

It simply identifies merchants whose observed chargebacks contain a relatively high proportion of fraud-related disputes.

---

## 🧠 Composite Risk Signal

I then combined the five individual signals into:

```text
risk_signal_count
```

For each merchant, this represents how many of the five risk conditions were triggered.

For example:

```text
High-risk score       = TRUE
High failure rate     = TRUE
High refund rate      = TRUE
High chargeback rate  = FALSE
Fraud-heavy           = TRUE

risk_signal_count = 4
```

This gives me a way to find merchants showing multiple independent warning signs.

---

## 🐍 Implementation

I created:

```text
python/analyze_risk_signals.py
```

The script performs the analysis without modifying the underlying transaction data.

The process is:

```text
Load merchants
      ↓
Process transactions in chunks
      ↓
Calculate merchant failure metrics
      ↓
Calculate refund metrics
      ↓
Calculate chargeback metrics
      ↓
Calculate fraud-related chargeback metrics
      ↓
Combine all metrics
      ↓
Create risk signals
      ↓
Calculate risk_signal_count
      ↓
Save merchant-level analysis
```

Because the transaction table contains 1 million records, I processed the transactions in chunks rather than loading the entire transaction table into memory at once.

## 📊 Example Findings

One example was:

```text
MERCH00192
Category: Insurance
Segment: SMB
Risk score: 55.74

Failure rate: 12.24%
Refund rate: 8.14%
Chargeback rate: 3.49%
Fraud chargeback rate: 33.33%

Risk signals: 4
```

What I found interesting here is that the merchant's predefined risk score is only **55.74**, which is below my high-risk threshold of 70.

However, the merchant still triggered four behavioral risk signals.

This demonstrates an important point for the project:

> **Baseline merchant risk and observed transaction behavior don't always tell the same story.**

This is useful for the later investigation layer because the system should be able to identify situations where actual behavior deserves attention even when a merchant's existing risk score doesn't look particularly high.

---

## 💡 Another Important Observation

There were:

```text
118 high-risk merchants
```

based on the predefined risk score, but:

```text
1,775 merchants
```

crossed the chargeback-rate threshold.

This shows that the different signals are capturing different aspects of merchant behavior.

I therefore decided not to collapse everything into one arbitrary "fraud score."

Instead, FinLeak keeps the individual evidence visible.

---

## 🐛 Problem Encountered

While initially trying to calculate merchant-level failure rates directly with SQL, MySQL returned:

```text
Error Code: 2013.
Lost connection to MySQL server during query
```

The query was taking around 30 seconds before the connection was lost.

I also tried considering an index-based solution, but I decided not to keep forcing a large aggregation query through MySQL.

---

## 🔧 Fix

I moved the heavy transaction-level aggregation into Python/Pandas.

The transactions were processed in chunks of:

```text
50,000 rows
```

This allowed me to process all 1 million transactions without loading the complete table into memory at once.

Refund and chargeback metrics were then combined with the transaction metrics at merchant level.

This approach also gives me more flexibility for the later analytics stages.

---

## ⚠️ Important Interpretation Rule

The risk signals are **investigation indicators**, not proof of fraud.

For example:

```text
High refund rate
```

doesn't automatically mean fraud.

Likewise:

```text
High chargeback rate
```

doesn't automatically mean the merchant is fraudulent.

The purpose of these signals is to answer:

> **"Which merchants should an analyst investigate further?"**

The actual explanation should come from supporting evidence.

This follows the main FinLeak principle:

> **Detect first. Explain second.**
